Persona panel · concept review · india market · 30 simulated customers

Kettlecraft (fictional example)

Monthly single-origin Indian tea & chai subscription, ₹799/month — fictional concept used as a public example

Nobody would buy yet: 0% buy, 57% no; the concept is understood (97%) but not trusted (2.1/5) or seen as good value (2.3/5).

  • The offer is clear to almost everyone, so the problem is confidence and price, not comprehension.
  • 24 of 30 personas flagged missing reviews, phone number, address or founder details; this is the biggest barrier, worst for tier-3 (trust 1.5) and regional-language shoppers (trust 1.71).
  • Rs 799 a month with no trial box or first-order discount stops price-sensitive and first-time buyers; all 5 COD-only personas said no.
  • Cash on delivery, per-cup pricing and richer product detail (photos, estates, tasting notes) are cheap fixes for the next most common objections.
  • Pause/cancel anytime and free India shipping were the most liked parts of the pitch.

What to fix, in order

1

No proof the brand is real (reviews, contact, address)

criticaleffort: medium phone
24 of 30 personas (80%) · trust

Who: 24 of 30 (80%) raised it; 4 blockers. Tier-3 trust 1.5, regional-language-first 1.71, skeptical 1.73 vs trusting 3.33.

Evidence: Personas saw an unknown brand with no reviews, phone number, address or return policy and called it risky for a first purchase.

Fix: Add a trust strip on the homepage: 5-10 real reviews, founder name and photo, phone/WhatsApp number, registered address, and a link to return and cancellation policy.

2

No cash on delivery or other payment options shown

criticaleffort: medium phone
13 of 30 personas (43%) · checkout

Who: All 5 COD-only personas said no (trust 1.6, ease 2.2); 7 COD-preferred also affected. Card/EMI user p030 also asked.

Evidence: Several personas said they will not prepay an unknown brand and found no mention of COD, cards or EMI.

Fix: Offer COD for the first order (or a small advance) and list UPI, card, EMI and COD with icons beside the price and at checkout.

higheffort: lowquick win phone
8 of 30 personas (27%) · pricing

Who: Comparison shoppers and wholesale or loose-tea buyers; 13 found the price pricey and 6 too expensive.

Method and caveats

Personas were sampled from weighted demographic distributions with coherence rules and guaranteed coverage of minority segments, then role-played by small AI models in batches. Panelists reacted to the written concept. These are simulated customers: they're good at surfacing friction, confusion and missing information, but they don't predict real conversion rates. Confirm the top themes with a handful of real customers before big changes.
